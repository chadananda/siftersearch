## Problem
The archive answers about one person at a time. The questions a scholar asks
are frequently about a *set*: which of the Letters of the Living have attested
pre-1844 contact with the Báb? Which recognition accounts follow the same
pattern? Where does the pattern break?

Chad's example: Mullá Ṣádiq's recognition in Kashan "was supposed to be
following the pattern of the letters of the living." That is a claim about a
narrative *shape* recurring across people — invisible to per-person retrieval,
however good.

## Why it depends on 0017
Patterns are compared between events. Without an encounter model there is
nothing to compare — only prose summaries, where similarity of wording is not
similarity of event.

## Wrong if
Pattern detection over sparse evidence invents patterns. Where only two or
three accounts exist, the honest output is the accounts and the observation
that the set is small — not a claimed regularity. **Reporting absence matters
as much as reporting the pattern**: which Letters have no attested pre-1844
contact is itself the finding.
