Anís as a research assistant in seven days

The target, the starting point, the numbers each stage must reach, the tests that decide them, and the day-by-day schedule.

Short answer. Seven days is enough for a demo that works on a fixed set of journeys and on the common question types. Reaching the same quality on any question a stranger types, scored by the judge at 8.5 of 10 across 100 conversations, takes about four weeks. Most of that time goes on turning real-use misses into tests and fixing them.

Where it stands today. On the 10-conversation test on 1 Nov (the answering pipeline production runs now), an Opus judge scored Anís 3.85 of 10. It found 108 defects, 11 of them critical, and 64 of the 108 were in the written answer. A new research agent (one model that searches the library and writes the answer, with code checking every citation) is built and tested, but it is switched off in production.

Risks and costs

  • Judge noise. One judge's score moves by about half a point from run to run. Each gate uses two judges, and a stage passes on the mean.
  • Testing cost. About $2 per 10 judged conversations (1 Nov actual). With roughly 30 conversations a day plus the Perplexity comparison, the week costs about $60–80 (estimate).
  • Answer cost. A stronger model for the hardest turns raises the cost per conversation. The 1 Nov cost was $0.015 per conversation, and I'll report the new figure each day. Today's figures don't support a reliable estimate of the new cost yet.
  • Search comes first. Batteries run through the gateway at a steady pace, so searches by real readers are never slowed by testing.
  • Breadth across traditions. I haven't measured yet how deep the other traditions' shelves go. Thursday's runs will show it; if a shelf is thin, Anís says so instead of covering it with web results.
